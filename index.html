FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

What does PauseScroll actually track about me?
Nothing leaves your device. Your intent text, limits, quiet hours and counters are stored only in your browser's local storage — and only used to render the app for you. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising identifiers.
Does it block other apps?
Not in the browser version — a web page cannot stop other apps from opening. PauseScroll runs its own timer inside the app and can only pause itself. System-wide blocking of other apps is planned for the Android APK, which can request accessibility permissions to close apps at your limit.
Will it drain my battery?
No. The timer stores an end timestamp and only wakes a few times per second while a session is running to redraw the ring. With no session active, nothing runs in the background at all.
Which platforms work?
The web app works in any modern browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari — on desktop and mobile. Installing as a standalone PWA works best on Android Chrome and desktop Chrome/Edge. iPhones and iPads install via Share → Add to Home Screen.
How much does it cost?
It's free. No trials, no subscriptions, no ads, no data for sale. It's a single calm page that works offline, and we'd like to keep it that way.
Where is my data stored?
In this browser, on this device, in localStorage. Export (a JSON file you download) and reset are available in Settings, and clearing your browser storage deletes everything.

Privacy

PauseScroll is a local, offline-first app. All data — your intents, limits, settings and counters — stays in your device's browser storage. We operate no server, keep no logs, and include no tracking scripts, analytics, or third-party requests of any kind.

Because your information never leaves your device, we have nothing to share, sell, or lose.

Terms

PauseScroll is provided as-is, for free, to help you use your time more deliberately. It is a tool for gentle self-limits, not a medical device and not a promise to change your habits.

The Android APK may request accessibility services so it can close other apps at your set limit. You grant that permission yourself, and the app only uses it while you're actively in a session.
